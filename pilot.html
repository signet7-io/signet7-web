Test one consequential workflow. Measure what people understand.

This pilot is a structured early-alpha evaluation, not a production service, customer contract, or public launch. Free access purchases disciplined learning: whether senders use Signet7 repeatedly, recipients verify, both sides understand what verification does and does not prove, and the workflow creates enough value to support a paid sender offering later.

First cohort

Freelancers and microbusinesses.

The first proposed direct cohort protects recurring invoices, payment instructions, contracts, scope changes, and bank-detail-change notices. These messages are frequent enough to test behavior and consequential enough to expose whether the evidence is useful.

Who fits

Freelancers, consultants, bookkeepers, contractors, and micro-agencies with a defined sender, recipient group, and recurring important-message workflow.

Who comes next

Title, legal, financial, medical, employment, insurance, and other institutional B2B2C partners that can sponsor free verification for recipients.

Who waits

Family and caregiver workflows wait for stronger managed identity, permission, recovery, support, and false-reassurance controls.

Pilot contract

Bounded in time, users, data, and promises.

01

Define

Write down one use case, dates, sender identities, recipient limits, allowed message classes, success metrics, stop criteria, and accountable owners before onboarding.

02

Start safely

Use simulated or lower-risk communications first. Do not rely on the alpha alone for live wire transfers, medical decisions, legal deadlines, credentials, secrets, or regulated personal data.

03

Learn weekly

Run assisted onboarding, observe recipient comprehension, review failures and support burden, and collect structured feedback. Testimonials remain voluntary and separately consented.

04

Measure

Track repeat sender use, verification completion, comprehension, unsupported-message rate, recovery/support demand, and stated willingness to pay.

05

Stop

Pause if recipients confuse verified with safe, unsupported transformations are common, signing custody is unreliable, or the workflow increases risk or support beyond the approved envelope.

06

Exit cleanly

No automatic paid conversion. At the end, participants receive an explicit continuation, export, extension, or closure decision.

What participants receive

Assisted access to the exact alpha.

The proposed pilot includes assisted setup, current supported signing and verification flows, free recipient checks where configured, verifier-response receipts, weekly feedback sessions, direct issue triage, and product-influence sessions.

Non-negotiable meaning

What verification does and does not prove.

Supported conclusion

  • A supported signature matched the exact protected representation under the reported key.
  • The verifier reports how the key was bound to the sender and its relevant lifecycle status.
  • Declared covered components are itemized.
  • A receipt can preserve the verifier response for later review.

Unsupported conclusion

  • The email is safe, truthful, lawful, malware-free, confidential, or prudent to act on.
  • The exact physical human typed or approved it.
  • Unprotected components, mailbox credentials, devices, or downstream payment details are trustworthy.
  • The receipt alone is an independent timestamp, external anchor, complete history, or legal-admissibility ruling.
Express interest
